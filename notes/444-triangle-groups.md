# The (4,4,4) complex hyperbolic triangle groups

## What they are, and the history with Rich Schwartz

A (4,4,4) complex hyperbolic triangle group is generated by three complex
reflections ι₁, ι₂, ι₃ of order 2 in three complex geodesics of CH², arranged
so that each pair of mirrors meets at angle π/4 — making every pairwise
product ιᵢιⱼ elliptic of order 4 (a rotation about the interior vertex). It is
the finite-vertex sibling of the Goldman–Parker *ideal* triangle groups, where
the vertices sit on the boundary sphere and the pairwise products are
parabolic instead.

The subject's arc runs through Rich's work. Goldman–Parker (1992) introduced
the ideal family and conjectured its discreteness range; Schwartz proved the
conjecture (Ann. of Math. 2001, with the streamlined proof in Geom. Topol.
2005 — `2005-35.tex` at the repo root), establishing the template: **a
one-parameter family of triangle-group representations stays a discrete
embedding exactly until a distinguished word element becomes elliptic.** His
ICM 2002 survey organized the general (p,q,r) landscape and singled out the
two governing words; for small triangles like (4,4,4) the governing word is

  **1213 = ι₁ι₂ι₁ι₃**

(note 1213 = (ι₁ι₂ι₁)·ι₃ is a product of two complex reflections — ι₁ι₂ι₁ is
the reflection in ι₁'s image of mirror 2 — so its type is literally the
geometry of those two mirrors: crossing ⟹ elliptic, asymptotic ⟹ parabolic).
The (4,4,4) family at and near the parameter where 1213 degenerates is the
setting of Rich's constructions of exotic spherical CR structures. In his
words (July 2026): *"When the element 1213 is parabolic or elliptic, the limit
set is the closure of a countable family of R-circles… a bit like circle
packing limit sets."* R-circles are boundaries of totally real geodesic
planes — the maximally non-holomorphic circles in S³.

## How we compute the family

The configuration is pinned by a Gram-matrix realization
(`src/examples/complex-hyperbolic/triangleGroup.ts`). Normalize the mirror
polar vectors to ⟨cᵢ,cᵢ⟩ = 1; the angle conditions fix the magnitudes
|⟨cᵢ,cⱼ⟩| = cos(π/4), and the single remaining modulus of the configuration is
the phase

  φ = arg( ⟨c₁,c₂⟩⟨c₂,c₃⟩⟨c₃,c₁⟩ ),

the angular invariant of the mirror triple — the mirror-side analogue of the
Cartan invariant that parameterizes the ideal family. Vectors realizing the
Gram matrix with the correct signature (2,1) exist iff det G < 0, which for
(4,4,4) bounds φ to the open interval **(π/4, 7π/4)**, and the realization is
a closed-form triangular solve (c₁ = e₁, c₂ in the first two coordinates, c₃
generic). Reflections are then R_ζ(z) = z + (ζ−1)(⟨z,c⟩/⟨c,c⟩)c with ζ = −1,
and the group is walked as a three-involution alphabet.

Landmarks on the interval: **φ = π is the R-Fuchsian point** (real Gram ⇒ the
classical hyperbolic (4,4,4) reflection group; limit set a round R-circle);
φ ↔ 2π−φ are complex-conjugate, mirror-image representations, so the honest
parameter range is [π, 7π/4). Everything is pinned by gates
(`scripts/tests/su21-triangle-gates.ts`): Gram exactness, vertex orders
exactly (4,4,4), reality at φ = π, interval endpoints.

## How we search for the parabolic and elliptic parameters of 1213

**Classification dial.** For M ∈ SU(2,1) with τ = tr M, Goldman's discriminant
f(τ) = |τ|⁴ − 8Re(τ³) + 18|τ|² − 27 is positive for loxodromic, negative for
regular elliptic, zero on the parabolic wall. We apply it to the word matrix
of 1213, det-normalized into SU(2,1) (`classifyElement` in `diagnostics.ts`).

**The parabolic point.** Moving from φ = π toward the wall, f(1213) starts
positive and crosses zero exactly once; `findParabolicPhase` bisects the sign
change:

  **φ\* ≈ 5.073755**  (f is flat at its zero, so only ~5 digits are
  well-conditioned; the geometry nearby is insensitive at that scale).

**The elliptic order-n ladder.** Past φ\*, the two colliding eigenvalues of
1213 separate along the unit circle by a growing *splitting angle* δ(φ)
(computed by Cardano on the characteristic cubic; `splittingAngle`). The
order-n parameter is where δ = 2π/n: `findEllipticPhase(orders, n)` bisects δ
and then **verifies** the projective order independently (`ellipticOrder`:
smallest k with Mᵏ scalar). For (4,4,4) the verified orders come out exactly
n, with n = 4 landing on the degenerate wall — so n ≥ 5 is the reachable
ladder, accumulating at φ\*:

| n | φ | | n | φ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 5.35320875 | | 10 | 5.14702036 |
| 6 | 5.27137885 | | 12 | 5.12491046 |
| 7 | 5.22066262 | | 16 | 5.10268916 |
| 8 | 5.18712849 | | 24 | 5.08666679 |

These are the parameters of Rich's R-circle-closure regime. Tools: the
`su21-triangle-groups` demo has "go to critical φ\*" and "go to 1213 elliptic
of order n" buttons; offline, `scripts/render/su21-tri-render-limit-set.ts
--critical | --order n` renders directly at the solved parameter, and
`scripts/research/su21-tri-rcircles.ts` / `su21-tri-rcircle-picture.ts` hunt
for and draw the certified R-circle families there.
